# Video Generation Prompt Engineering

Generative video represents the cutting edge of multimodal artificial intelligence. Models such as **OpenAI Sora**, **Runway Gen-3 Alpha**, **Kling 1.5**, and **Luma Dream Machine** can generate photorealistic, temporally coherent video clips from text and image prompts.

Unlike static image models, video prompts must account for **time, physical momentum, continuous illumination, and camera choreography**. If you only describe what is in the frame, the model will guess how things move—frequently resulting in morphing artifacts, unnatural physics, and floating appendages.

## The 5-Part Motion Prompt Architecture

To produce cinematic, artifact-free video clips, structure prompts around five sequential director's notes:

```text
1. Camera Choreography: Exact movement vector (e.g. FPV drone dive, slow tracking shot, dolly in, whip pan).
2. Subject Kinetic Action: What the subject is physically doing across the duration of the clip.
3. Spatial Environment & Atmosphere: Foreground, midground, background, weather elements, air particulates.
4. Lighting & Color Palette: Directional lighting continuity, color grade (e.g. Kodak 5219, teal-and-orange).
5. Temporal Dynamics & Physics: Wind speed, fluid motion, cloth ripple, dust kicked up, pacing velocity.
```

## The Director's Camera Movement Lexicon

AI video models respond strongly to cinematic camera terminology. Always specify the camera movement upfront:

### Dynamic Camera Motions
- **`Slow Tracking Shot / Trucking Shot`**: Camera travels parallel to the moving subject, maintaining consistent distance and framing.
- **`Dolly In / Push-In`**: Camera physically glides forward toward the subject, heightening intimacy, tension, or focus.
- **`Dolly Zoom / Vertigo Effect`**: Camera moves forward while optical zoom pulls back, warping background perspective while keeping subject scale constant.
- **`Orbital Arc / 360 Pan`**: Camera circles smoothly around the central subject, showcasing three-dimensional volume and lighting interaction.
- **`FPV Drone Dive`**: Fast, exhilarating descent from high altitude through tight physical spaces, pulling up smoothly near ground level.
- **`Crane / Jib Shot`**: Sweeping vertical elevation starting from a low subject close-up and rising to reveal a grand landscape panorama.

## The Multi-Model Production Pipeline (The Industry Standard)

<Callout type="tip">
**Why Pure Text-to-Video Fails in Commercial Work**: Pure text-to-video introduces character face drifting, inconsistent wardrobe, and random background styles across takes. 
Enterprise teams **never** generate final video purely from text.
</Callout>

To achieve complete commercial control, follow the **Two-Stage Multi-Model Pipeline**:

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    A[Step 1: Concept & Composition] -->|Generate Master Keyframe| B[FLUX.1 Pro / Midjourney v6.1]
    B -->|Export Flawless 16:9 Image| C[Step 2: Motion Animation]
    C -->|Upload as 'First Frame'| D[Runway Gen-3 Alpha / Kling 1.5]
    D -->|Apply Motion-Only Prompt| E[Final Photorealistic 4K Video Clip]
```

### Stage 1: Lock the Keyframe
Generate your master frame using **FLUX.1 Pro** or **Midjourney v6.1** until character anatomy, wardrobe, branding, and lighting are 100% locked.

### Stage 2: Prompt for Camera Motion Only
Upload the generated image as the **First Frame** in Runway Gen-3 Alpha, and provide a motion prompt that directs *only* the movement:

```text
[First Frame Image Uploaded]
Camera performs a slow, cinematic push-in. The subject turns her head smoothly to make direct eye contact with the lens and offers a subtle, confident smile. Soft breeze catches loose strands of her hair. Golden sunlight glints off the glass skyscraper windows in the background. Photorealistic human movement, natural blink rate, cinematic 24fps motion.
```

## Avoiding Video Artifacts: Negative Directives

When prompting video generation engines, prevent common distortion anomalies with these physics rules:

- **Morphing Prevention**: Explicitly state *"Subject maintains structural geometry; no melting, morphing, or disappearing limbs."*
- **Speed Consistency**: Specify pacing (*"Slow, measured cinematic pace; no sudden robotic acceleration"*).
- **Temporal Consistency**: Define persistent objects (*"The car remains fully intact and visible throughout the entire clip"*).
